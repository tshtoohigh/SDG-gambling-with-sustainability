Where the cards come from

Bought as collections, graded one at a time

Card shops and estate clearances end up with binders nobody has hours to sort. A thousand cards might contain four worth listing individually, and finding those four costs more in labour than they'd earn. So the whole binder gets sold on by weight. Same economics as a clothing bale, same reason we can buy it.

Every card is then checked by hand: centring, surface, edges, corners. Anything counterfeit, water-damaged or creased through the art goes to card recycling, not into a box. We grade conservatively — if a card sits between two grades we assign the lower one, because the incentive has to run that direction.

On authenticity

Everything we sell is genuine secondhand product. We don't print, reproduce or "inspire-by" anybody's cards, and we don't sell proxies. If we can't verify a card is authentic, it doesn't ship — it gets destroyed and logged.

The pixel-art illustrations on this site are our own original designs, used to show what each rarity band looks like structurally. They are not depictions of any particular card or franchise. What arrives in your box is real product from real secondhand stock.

Is this gambling?

We take the question seriously here more than anywhere else on the site, because randomised card products are exactly where this gets sharp. Our answer is the same as for clothing: the odds are published and versioned, the worst outcome still has real use value, there's no cash-out and no resale market we operate, and we run none of the mechanics that make these things compulsive — no timers, no streaks, no paid re-rolls with a downside, no artificial scarcity.

We also don't sell card boxes to anyone under 18, and there's a one-box-per-month cap on subscriptions. Read the full answer
