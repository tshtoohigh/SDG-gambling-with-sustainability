Anatomy of a box

Where the randomness actually is

Only the feature slots are rolled. The everyday staples aren't a lottery — they're guaranteed wearable basics, chosen to your size. That means the floor of every box is useful clothing, not filler.

Feature slots

Rolled independently against the published table. Two slots means two separate chances — not one chance doubled. A sorter draws from the graded rare rail to match the ratios, and we audit the realised rates monthly.

Everyday staples

No roll, no rarity. Solid basics in your size that were heading for a bale: merino jumpers, straight-leg denim, unpilled cotton tees. This is the majority of the weight we divert, and honestly the part that gets worn most.

Where this comes from

Bought by weight, sorted by hand

A thrift store can only hang a fraction of what it's donated. The rest gets baled and sold on by the pound — usually for export or shredding. That's the stream we buy from, and it's why a $10 box can contain something genuinely good: the garment was never priced individually in the first place.

Every bale is opened on a bench and graded piece by piece. Roughly a third of what we buy doesn't make it into a box — it goes to textile recycling instead. We'd rather tell you that than pretend our yield is perfect.

  • Holes, stains, heavy pilling textile recycling, never a box.
  • Wearable basics everyday staple slots.
  • Pre-2000s, labelled, or unusual graded onto the feature rail.
